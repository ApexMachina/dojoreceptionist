AI Receptionist vs Answering Service: Which Fits a Small Shop?

AI receptionist vs answering service is a choice about who picks up when you cannot. A live answering service puts a person on the line. An AI receptionist is software that answers the same way each time and sends you what the caller said. For a small owner-run shop, a plumber, an HVAC crew, an auto shop, a salon, or a cleaner, pick by the calls you get. A messy story, a live transfer, or a payment on the call belongs with a person. A name, a number, and the job can sit with an AI receptionist. You still call the customer back.

What a live answering service does well

A person can follow your script and still change course when the caller leaves it. They can transfer the call, book a time you have already allowed, and on some plans take a payment before they hang up. That is the right tool for an urgent job, a caller who will not leave a recording, or a story you never scripted.

As of November 26, 2026, Smith.ai's human plans are live-staffed 24/7, with no setup fee, and spam calls are free when caller ID passes through. Starter is 30 calls for $300 a month, then $11.50 per extra call. The largest published plan is 300 calls for $2,100 a month. A Spanish line is $1.00 per call, booking is $1.50 per call, and taking a payment is $1.00 per call. Plans are month to month. New clients get a 30-day money-back guarantee, capped at $1,000, with overage excluded.

AnswerConnect's pricing PDF says the receptionists are real people and that the company uses no AI agents. Entry is 200 minutes for $350 a month plus a $49.99 setup fee, then $2.50 a minute. Growth is 300 minutes for $395 a month, and that card says no setup fee, then $1.85 a minute. Calls round up, so 2 minutes and 10 seconds bills as 3. Spanish answering is listed at $30. A later page of the same PDF says every plan includes the $49.99 setup fee, which does not match the Growth card. Ask which line applies before you pay.

What an AI receptionist does well

An AI receptionist picks up at night, on weekends, and while you are on a job. The questions stay the same, so the note is steadier than a voicemail you play in the truck. A set call count is easier to predict than minutes that grow with a long conversation.

Smith.ai publishes an AI plan beside the human one. The free plan is $0 a month for 25 calls, then $3.00 per extra call. Pro is $150 a month for 75 calls at $2.00 each, with extra calls on that tier at $2.50. Enterprise is $500 a month for 300 calls at $1.67 each. Plans include 24/7 handling, recording, transcription, and summaries. Canceling needs 30 days' notice. The page says a complex call can move to a live receptionist.

Where each one falls short

Past the included calls or minutes, the bill climbs. Fifteen calls over Smith.ai's 30-call human Starter plan are 15 times $11.50, or $172.50 on top of the $300. AnswerConnect rounds minutes up, and add-ons sit outside the plan. Ruby's page says there is no fee for activation, onboarding, or setup, that answering is live 24/7, and that Spanish answering is available. Optional AI enhancements cost nothing extra there. Minute prices for the receptionist were not on the page. Live chat starts at $143 a month for 10 chats. The post on what an answering service costs walks through more of these bills.

An AI receptionist stays on its script. It should refuse to quote a price or to diagnose a noise, a leak, or a smell, because a confident wrong answer sounds like the shop said it. Some callers hang up on a synthetic voice. Spam rules differ too. Smith.ai's human plans skip spam when caller ID passes through. Its AI plans count each answered call, allow up to 10 percent to be removed as spam, and filter known spam numbers before the AI picks up.

Questions to ask any vendor

Ask these before you sign, whether the vendor is a live service or an AI product. The answers belong on the pricing page or in the contract.

  • Do you bill by the minute or by the call, and how do you round?
  • What does one call or one minute past the plan cost?
  • Is there a setup fee, and do the plan card and the fine print match?
  • Are spam, robocalls, and hang-ups billed?
  • Can you transfer the call live, or do you only take a message?
  • Will you quote a price or guess what is wrong? For a shop, the answer should be no.
  • How does the message reach me, and how fast?
  • Do you answer in the languages my callers use, and is that included or an add-on?
  • Is there a contract or a notice period to cancel?
  • Can I hear the greeting before I pay?

Count a week of missed calls before you pick a plan. The 7-Day Missed Call Log is a paper way to do that, and the comparison page lines up voicemail, a live service, and an AI.
