What Does an Answering Service Cost for a Small Business?

A live answering service for a small business usually costs a few hundred dollars a month. Published plans we checked on November 24, 2026 start at $300 a month for 30 calls (Smith.ai) and $350 a month for 200 minutes (AnswerConnect). The starting price is only part of it. How the plan counts your usage, what an overage costs, setup fees, and per-call add-ons decide what you actually pay.

Per minute or per call: the two ways you get billed

Per minute. AnswerConnect sells monthly buckets of minutes. Its Entry plan is 200 minutes for $350 a month plus a $49.99 setup fee, with extra minutes at $2.50 each. Growth is 300 minutes for $395 with no setup fee, and Standard is 400 minutes for $575 plus the $49.99 setup fee; extra minutes on those two are $1.85. Calls are rounded up to the next full minute, so a call that lasts 2 minutes and 10 seconds bills as 3. The first 30 calls under 30 seconds each billing cycle are free.

Per call. Smith.ai's live receptionist plans count calls, not minutes. Starter is 30 calls for $300 a month, then $11.50 per extra call. Basic is 90 calls for $810 ($10.50 per extra call), and Pro is 300 calls for $2,100 ($8.50 per extra call). It lists no setup fee, does not charge for spam calls that pass caller ID, runs month to month, and offers a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Which model is cheaper depends on your callers. If they talk a long time, a per-call plan is kinder. If most calls are a quick "are you open Saturday," a minute bucket stretches further.

Add-ons change the bill

The plan price usually covers answering and taking a message. Extra work on the call is often priced per call. Smith.ai lists booking an appointment at $1.50 a call, a text or email follow-up to the caller at $0.50, call recording and transcription at $0.25, a dedicated Spanish line at $1.00, and taking a payment at $1.00. Showing your business number on outbound calls is $10 a month. If you want every one of 30 calls booked and recorded, that is another $52.50 on top of the $300.

Ruby takes a different approach. Its pricing page says there are no added fees for activation, onboarding, setup, customization, or coverage at certain hours. As of November 24, 2026 its virtual receptionist prices are not posted on that page; you build a plan with its sales team. Its live chat plans are posted, starting at $143 a month for 10 chats.

Work out your own number

Here is the math with example numbers, not yours. Say a shop gets 45 calls a month that it cannot answer.

  • On Smith.ai Starter, that is $300 plus 15 extra calls at $11.50, or $472.50 a month.
  • On AnswerConnect Entry, if those calls average 3 billed minutes, that is 135 minutes, inside the 200: $350 a month plus the one-time $49.99 setup fee.
  • If the same calls average 5 billed minutes, that is 225 minutes, 25 over: $350 plus $62.50, or $412.50 a month.

To run it for your own shop, count the calls you miss for one week. The free 7-Day Missed Call Log is a simple way to do that. Multiply by about 4.3 for a month, guess how long a typical call runs, and price that against each plan's overage, not just its starting number.

Compare it with hiring someone

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median pay for receptionists at $18.27 an hour, or $38,010 a year, as of May 2025. That works out to about $3,168 a month in wages, before payroll taxes, benefits, and time off. A person also covers only their shift, while the services above advertise 24/7 answering. A hire makes sense when you need someone at the counter too. If you only need the phone covered, a service costs a fraction of a salary.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Do you bill by the minute or by the call, and how do you round?
  • What does each call or minute over the plan cost?
  • Is there a setup fee, a contract, or a notice period to cancel?
  • Are spam, robocalls, and wrong numbers billed?
  • Which add-ons will I actually use, and what does each cost per call?
  • Can I hear how you answer before I pay?

When something cheaper is enough

If most calls can wait an hour, a clear voicemail greeting with a promised callback window costs nothing extra. An AI receptionist is another option, usually a flat monthly fee. It answers every call the same way and should tell callers it is an AI, but it is software: it will not think on its feet the way a trained person can, and it should not quote prices or diagnose problems.
