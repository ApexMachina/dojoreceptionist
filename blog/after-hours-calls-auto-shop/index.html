How to Handle After-Hours Calls as an Auto Shop

Handle after-hours calls at an auto shop by saying, in the greeting, that you are closed and when you will call back. Write down the name, number, vehicle, and what the caller noticed, then return the call in a morning window you already blocked. If someone might be hurt, or a strong fuel smell is filling a closed garage, tell them to get to fresh air and call 911. A small owner-run shop can do this on the phone it already has.

Record a greeting that names the callback window

"Leave a message" does not say when you will call back. Record one you will keep: "Thanks for calling the shop. We are closed. We return calls at the start of the next business morning, before the first car goes on the lift. Leave your name, a callback number, the vehicle, and what you noticed. If someone is hurt, hang up and call 911." "As soon as possible" sends people to the next shop. Voicemail usually comes with the line. Record it, call the shop from your cell, and listen as a new customer. Score what you heard on the Call Your Own Shop page, and put the same window on the door.

Forward the shop line to a cell, with rules

Call forwarding sends the shop line to another phone when you do not pick up. After hours, use your cell or the cell of whoever agreed to carry it, and only on nights that person will answer. A phone put away is just a second voicemail. If nobody will answer overnight, skip forwarding and play the greeting. Test after you close, from another phone: the cell should ring, and the greeting should be the after-hours one. At opening, turn the night rule off. Write which nights roll to a cell, which go to the greeting, and who owns the morning list.

Put the real hours on your Google Business Profile

People read the listing before they dial. If it says open and you are closed, or closed while you are still answering, they call someone else. Set a normal week on Google's edit-hours page, which says those hours show on Maps and Search. Check the listing while signed out of the shop account.

For a holiday or other short change, use special hours so the regular week stays put. Google's special-hours page says you can set them when hours change or you stay closed for up to 6 days in a row, and that holiday hours are worth confirming even when they match a normal week. The edit-hours page states that limit as 6 days in a row or fewer. For 7 days in a row or longer, or a closure with no known end, the special-hours page says to mark the profile temporarily closed. Special hours show only if the profile is set to open with main hours.

Separate a true emergency from next-morning work

Put the same split in the greeting so the whole shop gives one answer. Next-morning work, such as a noise or a car that will not start where it is parked, gets a message and a callback. Do not name a cause you have not seen, because a guess on a recording sounds like a diagnosis. Someone who might be hurt, or a strong fuel smell in a closed garage, should get to fresh air and call 911. Do not talk them through a repair. A car already in a bay is a morning callback too, unless someone really answers at night. If you do not work at night, say so.

Hold a morning callback block

Before the first car goes on a lift, block time only for last night's calls. One person owns a pad or a shared note with the name, number, vehicle, and what they noticed. Mark it done when you reach them. If you miss them, try once more inside the window. Leave card numbers off the pad, and take any deposit on a system you control when you call back. Clear a full mailbox before the block ends. If the list outlasts the block, change the greeting or the block before you add a tool. The auto repair missed-call calculator uses the numbers you type.

When a person or an AI should take the message

When messages sit past the window, or nobody can reach a phone for a long stretch, add a live answering service or an AI receptionist. The comparison page sets voicemail, a live service, and an AI side by side. Choose a live service for a long talk or a transfer to the cell on duty, and read the company's current plan. Published examples are in the post on what an answering service costs. An AI receptionist should say it is an AI, take the name, number, and what they need, and send a written summary. It should not quote a price or diagnose. You still call back. More notes for owner-run shops are on the blog.
